Current focus · LLM post-training

Sharpen or Expand? RLVR post-training study

2026 – present

Independent research · Texas A&M FASTER GPU cluster

Does reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards sharpen what a reasoning model already solves (higher pass@1), or expand what it can solve at all (higher pass@k at large k)?

Study design
  • Qwen3.5-4B-Base, starting from one frozen SFT checkpoint; compares DPO, GRPO, Dr.GRPO, DAPO and RS-GRPO at equal rollout-token budgets.
  • A frozen, hash-pinned evaluation suite: MATH-500, AIME 2024/2025 and OlympiadBench, a held-out split, and a contamination-resistant set. The RL data pool is decontaminated against all three.
  • Unbiased pass@k for k up to 64, plus problem-level transition tables (solved → unsolved and back).
Engineering
  • Built on verl, vLLM and TRL with FSDP2, run under Slurm and Apptainer; measured LoRA vs. full-BF16 memory and throughput on 4×A40.
  • Fault-tolerant runs: a killed job resumes with matching metrics. HF export merges LoRA in FP32, and identity runs reproduce the base model bit for bit.
  • Found and fixed framework issues: checkpoint pruning across resumes, Dr.GRPO loss normalization under dynamic batching, and a weight-sync crash for tensors over 2 GB.

Pilot result (Qwen3.5-2B GRPO, 37 steps): training reward rose from 0.33 to 0.48 (5.9 standard errors). Pass@1 rose from 0.41 to 0.53 on the standard benchmarks and from 0.40 to 0.59 on the held-out split. Part of the gain comes from fewer truncated answers, which is exactly the kind of confound the full 4B study is designed to separate.
